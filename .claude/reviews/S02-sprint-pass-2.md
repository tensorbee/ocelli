## The loop signal, recorded for the next sprint

F-010's review loop ran thirteen passes over roughly five hours and oscillated
rather than converged: 20, 9, 5, 7, 4, 3, 4, 2, 5, 5, 5, 3, 2 blocking items.
The cause was structural. The diff grew from +5762 to +10321 during the loop,
because each remediation added guards, tests and prose, and that new surface is
what the next pass found defects in. From pass 9 the dominant finding was the
same shape every round: the previous round's fix was watched by nothing, or a
check reported green having run nothing.

It was closed by changing the strategy rather than by more passes: freeze the
surface, sweep the whole class of guards once instead of one member per round,
and write the prose last against a frozen tree.

**The sweep immediately justified itself.** It found that the mutation harness
used in the earlier rounds was itself broken, because `node --test tests/`
treats the directory as a test and fails before running anything, so every
earlier "all refusals red" result had a red baseline and proved nothing. Re-run
correctly against a 137-pass baseline, six of 26 refusals in `src/` were
watched by nothing. Five gained tests and the sixth was fired by hand. Nothing
was deleted for being untestable.

That is the sprint's most transferable lesson and it is why **F-X009** exists:
a guard mutated in the same command that adds it has been observed to fire
once, and has nothing watching it afterwards.
